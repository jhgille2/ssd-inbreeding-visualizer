Meiosis: where the mosaic comes from

Each homolog of the plant above was delivered by one gamete, and each gamete was produced by one meiosis in the parent plant. Advancing a generation replays one of those meioses: watch the homologs pair, cross over, physically exchange segments, and segregate — only the highlighted gamete is transmitted to the next plant.

How to read this figure

  • Homolog 1 / Homolog 2 are the two copies of one chromosome in a single plant. Blue blocks trace back to Parent A, amber blocks to Parent B. A block boundary inside a homolog is the footprint of a crossover.
  • Genotype combines the two homologs: solid blue or amber where the plant is homozygous, split blue/amber where it is still heterozygous (upper half = Homolog 1 allele, lower half = Homolog 2 allele).
  • × marks show where crossovers occurred in the meiosis that produced each gamete. Hover over one for its position in centimorgans.
  • Crossovers keep happening every generation, but once a region is homozygous they leave no visible trace — recombining two identical homologs changes nothing.

Why repeated selfing produces inbred lines

The F1 is heterozygous at every locus that differed between the parents. When it is selfed, each locus segregates 1:2:1, so half the progeny genome — in expectation — becomes homozygous. Selfing again halves the remaining heterozygosity, and so on: F2 ~50%, F3 ~75%, F4 ~87.5%, approaching 100% asymptotically. Recombination does not create this homozygosity; it only reshuffles which parental segments sit together on each chromosome, producing the mosaic pattern. That is why two inbred lines from the same cross can both be ~99% homozygous yet carry different combinations of parental segments — the raw material a breeder selects among.

Single-seed descent means advancing the population by taking exactly one seed from each plant each generation, with no selection. It is fast, cheap, and preserves the population's genetic variance, at the cost of giving selection nothing to act on until lines are nearly fixed.

Model & assumptions

  • One representative 100 cM chromosome with 1,000 evenly spaced markers. Soybean has 20 chromosome pairs (2n = 40); every chromosome pair in the plant follows the same process.
  • Parents are fully homozygous and fixed for contrasting alleles at every marker, so allele identity equals parental origin at every position.
  • Meiosis follows the Haldane model: crossover counts are Poisson with mean equal to the chromosome length in Morgans, positions are uniform, and there is no crossover interference.
  • Each selfing uses two independent meioses in the parent; one random chromatid is transmitted per gamete (the single seed). No selection, no mutation, no segregation distortion.
  • The default dataset was generated with scripts/generate_data.py, a direct port of the AlphaSimR reference implementation in R/simulate_ssd.R. Pressing “Simulate a new line” re-runs the same model in your browser with a fresh random seed, so the wobble of individual lines around the expectation can be explored.
The committed dataset is one realized lineage (seed 174), chosen because its homozygosity trajectory tracks the expectation closely. Any single line will deviate by chance; the expectation holds over many lines.
